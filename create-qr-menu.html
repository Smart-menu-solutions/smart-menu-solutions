How to create a QR menu, step by step

From paper menu to QR code on the table: which options there are, what to prepare and what matters for the QR code itself.

Guides · 30 September 2026 · about 5 minutes to read

A QR menu is easy to explain: guests scan a code on the table and see your menu on their phone, no app needed. For it to work well and still be right months later, a little preparation pays off. This guide walks you through the steps.

Step 1: Choose your approach

There are essentially three options:

  • A PDF behind a QR code: the simplest option. The code opens your existing menu as a PDF. On a phone, though, guests have to zoom a lot, and every change needs a new file.
  • A do-it-yourself builder: you type dishes and prices into an online tool yourself. That takes time, especially with several languages.
  • A full service: you send your menu, a provider sets up the digital menu and makes changes for you.

What each option costs is covered in How much does a digital QR menu cost?

Step 2: Prepare your menu

Whichever approach you choose, a current and complete starting point saves corrections later.

  • Current prices and dishes: remove anything you no longer serve before the menu goes digital.
  • Clear categories: for example starters, mains, desserts, drinks. A clear structure helps especially on a phone.
  • Short descriptions: the main ingredients are enough. Hardly anyone reads long texts on a phone.
  • Required information: details such as allergens and additives belong on the digital menu just as on the printed one.

Step 3: Decide on languages

Think about where your guests come from. English almost always makes sense, plus the languages of your most frequent guests. With a digital menu one QR code is enough, guests choose their language in the menu. More in Multilingual menus.

Step 4: Place the QR code well

  • Easy to see: on every table, for example as a table stand or sticker, and ideally at the entrance too.
  • Not too small: guests should be able to scan it easily from where they sit.
  • Good contrast: a dark code on a light background scans most reliably.
  • A short hint: a line like "Scan for the menu" helps guests who haven't used one before.

Step 5: Test it

Before going live, scan the code with several phones, ideally an iPhone and an Android device, right at the table. Check that all categories, prices and languages are correct. If the signal in your venue is weak, guest Wi-Fi helps.

Step 6: Keep it up to date

The big advantage of a digital menu is that it can change without reprinting the QR code. Make use of it: new prices, seasonal dishes or sold-out items should show up in the menu quickly.

Conclusion

A QR menu is quick to create when the groundwork is right: a current menu, clear categories, the right languages and a clearly visible QR code. After that, what matters is keeping it up to date. That is exactly its advantage over paper.
